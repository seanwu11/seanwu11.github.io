04 — Match Worn

A dissertation and applied marketing plan on why luxury-sport partnerships succeed or fail with Gen Z men — and a campaign concept built to answer it.

The Problem

Luxury fashion has been losing consumers for three years running, and Generation Z men — the fastest-growing spenders in the category — remain the hardest group to reach. Football and Formula 1 have become the industry's go-to answer, but not every brand partnership actually works.

The Research

I surveyed 80 Gen Z men who follow football or F1, testing a model of how a sport partnership moves someone from simply noticing a brand to actually wanting to buy from it. The finding that mattered most: noticing a collaboration barely shifts how someone feels about the brand on its own — that's the weakest link in the whole chain. But once perception genuinely changes, it converts into purchase intent very strongly. Awareness alone isn't the win; earned belief is.

A Dior × Mbappé pairing served as a live counter-example in the data — high-profile, low cultural fit, and it barely moved intent at all.

Consumers can tell the difference between cultural coherence and commercial convenience.

The Applied Concept

Using those findings, I built a full marketing plan for a proposed Maison Margiela × Michael Olise collaboration. Both are known for saying very little and communicating entirely through the work itself — the cultural fit already exists, rather than being built by a marketing team.

The campaign, "Match Worn," centres on the idea of a shirt that carries real evidence of having been played in — authenticity that no replica can fake — and extends that logic into a small capsule collection.
